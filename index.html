4 · Speak ⭐ Two-Person Dialogue

Choose one of the three situations below. Then create and speak both parts of the dialogue — say each speaker’s lines out loud, one after the other. Use the target grammar and vocabulary for the option you chose.

A · Misunderstanding in the Workplace — you and a colleague on the same team work through a misunderstanding and agree how to move forward, while keeping a healthy working relationship.
Grammar: discourse markers (narrowing the focus, softening a counter-argument, signaling accurate information, pivoting to action) and pragmatic softeners (distancing, softening disagreement, framing requests as gentle questions with past continuous, highlighting value before a request).
Vocabulary: ambiguous, bottleneck, constructive, convoluted, discrepancy, jump to conclusions, misalignment, misinterpret, oversight, proactive, rectify, resolution, touch base.

B · Parent–Teacher Partnership — a parent and a teacher hold a conference to solve an issue and build a plan to help the child succeed.
Grammar: advanced modals for accountability and problem-solving, and indirect questions for strategic politeness.
Vocabulary: academic progress, attendance, behavioral, conference, curriculum, disciplinary, intervention, missing work, ownership, proficiency, rubric, syllabus, tardiness.

C · Side Hustle — a freelancer and a client discuss expanding the scope of the work. The freelancer politely avoids working for free and tries to upsell.
Grammar: First and Second Conditionals to set professional boundaries politely, plus infinitives of purpose and gerunds as subjects to highlight customer benefit.
Vocabulary: bundle, comprehensive, deliverable, gig economy, incentive, maximize, premium, project scope, retainer, scope creep, throw in, tier, value-add.
